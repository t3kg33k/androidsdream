If you're actively following my blog you'll notice a rare occurrence... two post in a row. Yep. I've been motivated. And have had a little extra time lately. So here goes my thoughts on notes.

In the last fifteen years or so I have really made a conscience effort of taking notes as a daily practice. This is a vast contrast to how I treated note taking in my younger years; I was horrible, even in high school in the 80s and college in the 90s. I think the reason I have changed my practices is that I have found with age progression that I forget things more frequently. For a long time I jumped around on services. In the early years of Evernote, I used it for a bit and then went over to Google Keep. I was a long time user of Google Keep but then noticed a trend with Google of killing their services too often. Since I left that service around 2021 I have been jumping around trying to find the best notes service for my needs. A service that I can easily open my smartphone, start jotting down notes and still be accessible, after a sync, for when I get home and can either access the notes from a web interface or a desktop app on my Linux desktop and laptop. That led to trying Joplin and Obsidian for a short while. Those apps are actually pretty awesome but there were paper cuts, at that time, with the sync process and being able to access my notes on multiple devices.

Fast forward to 2024. I had been using ProtonMail for a while and got the news that Proton had acquired [Standard Notes](https://standardnotes.com), an open source and secure-minded notes service. What also peaked my interest was the fact that I could get a pretty deep discount on the service for being a Proton user. So, I signed up. Since then it has been an absolutely wonderful service. You can take rich notes, markdown, code, spreadsheet and even Excalidraw. Also, the needed option of being able to embed files like images and PDF is a major bonus for me. Of course it also has backup/restore options and the ability to export most of your notes as markdown files. It really is pretty amazing for the price. Two issues, however, that I have found recently with more use of Standard Notes is that I don't like the fact that I can't have a tree-like view to nest notes and with the paid plan that I have now I am quickly running out of space. I think it is time to start searching elsewhere for a different service.

Enter [Trilium Notes](https://triliumnotes.org). I just discovered this service last night and with further research I think I have found a possible candidate to replace Standard Notes. All the features listed on their site and Github seems to tick most of my boxes. And one of the best parts is that I can self-host it as a Docker container and just use it on my Tailscale network for accessing its web interface while away from my home. If I find that the web interface doesn't suite my needs on my mobile devices, there are also Android, iOS, and Linux app versions available. I think I am going to try this out for a bit to see if it is a good replacement for Standard Notes. I'll try to post my experiences in a later post.

If you want further information on Trilium Notes one of the resources I found useful was this [YouTube video here](https://youtu.be/bdB3LJvyokI?si=ZLOaymfN_2s256dz).
